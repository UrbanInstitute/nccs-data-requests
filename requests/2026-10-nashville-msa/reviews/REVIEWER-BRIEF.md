# Nashville nonprofit data request, round 2: brief for the reviewer

Prepared 2026-10-22. Requester deadline 2026-11-13; planned delivery 2026-11-09.

## Background

A colleague in the Research to Action Lab is working with Metro Nashville and the local community foundation on the city's nonprofit sector. In early October I sent them a first memo (attached) describing Nashville nonprofits with IRS data: counts and locations from the Business Master File, and finances and workforce from full Form 990 e-filings, tax years 2019 to 2023. The memo covers Davidson County (the consolidated city) and the 14-county metro, compared with 19 peer metros and the rest of the country. Hospitals and universities are excluded throughout.

Their team read the memo and returned comments (attached). Most were requests for charts or finer detail. They also made two new asks. This round covers all of it.

## What is being asked

Charts from the existing data. New organizations by year in Davidson County, with an exits series if it can be built. The distribution of operating expenses by percentile, to show how small most organizations are. Government grants as a share of revenue by year, Davidson against peers and the US, so the pandemic-era peak is visible. One optional chart of filers, inflation-adjusted spending, and employees over the five years.

A profile of about 100 organizations. The team keeps a map of the organizations they consider the core of the local ecosystem. They want, for that group: age, starts and exits, median operating expenses, government grant share, inflation-adjusted spending, and reported employees, compared with Davidson charities as a whole. They are sending names and addresses, not EINs, so there is a matching step; unmatched names go back to them.

Who funds whom. Grants reported by Davidson-based funders on their own returns (Schedule I for public charities, the grants-paid schedule for private foundations), 2019 to 2023, matched to the same list. The purpose is to see which funders support which mapped organizations. This covers funders based in Davidson only, so funders elsewhere are absent by construction.

## Measures

Size is measured by total expenses (full 990 Part I line 18, 990-EZ line 17), not revenue. Government grants are full 990 Part VIII line 1e, which is grants only, not contracts or reimbursements; blank is treated as zero. Employees are full 990 Part I line 5. Age is the IRS ruling date, which is recognition, not founding. Dollar figures are adjusted to 2023 with CPI-U. Employees and government grants exist only on the full 990, so 990-EZ and 990-N filers show blanks on those measures; the profile will report how many organizations fall in each form type.

## Where I would like your judgment

Exits. The Business Master File does not record when an organization leaves. I can date an exit either from annual BMF snapshots (first year absent; covers everyone but steps at snapshot years) or from the last 990 filed (cleaner timing but covers filers only). Round 1 showed that about half of apparent leavers were filing lag or e-file coverage gaps. My proposal is to use last-990-filed through 2022 as the main series, cross-checked against the merged CORE files, show the snapshot method as a check, and say plainly that exits in 2023 and later cannot be read yet.

Comparator for the profile. All Davidson charities, or only full-990 filers of similar size and field? I propose both, with all charities as the headline, since the question is where the mapped organizations sit in the sector.

990-EZ filers in the profile. Include them with blanks on employees and grants, or drop them? I propose including them and reporting coverage by form type for each measure.

Funder table. Schedule I only itemizes recipients receiving more than $5,000 in the year, and grants made through donor-advised funds appear under the sponsoring organization. I will report shared funders as shared funders, not as duplication, and an organization with no matched grant as "no matched grant observed", not as a gap. I would like your view on whether that framing is enough.

## Caveats carried from round 1

Filings lag about three years, so 2023 is the latest full year and nothing after it can be read. Current addresses are applied to history. The e-file data and the CORE files each miss a few percent of the other's 2023 full-990 filings, so 2019 to 2023 changes are shown alongside 2019 to 2022.
